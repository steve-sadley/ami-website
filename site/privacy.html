Privacy notice

What AMI holds about you, why it holds it, how long it keeps it, who else sees it, and what you can ask it to do. Written to be read rather than to be complied with.

Two things in this notice are more consequential than the rest, so they are at the top rather than buried where nobody reaches them.

AMI keeps membership and CPD records indefinitely. It does not delete them when a membership lapses and it does not delete them after a fixed number of years. The reason is in the section on keeping records below, and you are entitled to disagree with it and to object.

If you are a current member, some of what AMI holds is published. Your name, your membership number and your grade appear on the public register and on the page a scanned membership card leads to. Your employer, your joining date and your photograph appear there too, and each of those you control. The section on the public register says exactly which is which.

Who is responsible

The Asbestos Management Institute is the data controller for the information described here. Written enquiries about this notice, and any request under the rights set out below, should go to the office.

AMI to confirm before launch: the registered address, the Information Commissioner's Office registration number, and the named contact for data protection enquiries. This notice has not yet been reviewed by a solicitor and should be before the site goes live.

What AMI holds, and why

Your application. Your name, date of birth, private address, telephone number, email address, employer, job title, the date you were appointed to it, the invoicing details you gave, and the documents you attached, which are typically a CV, training certificates, qualifications and a CSCS card. AMI holds these because it cannot assess an application for membership without them. Nobody outside AMI sees your application or your documents.

Your professional interview. If you are invited to the interview that forms part of joining, you book a time on this site. Your name and email address then go into the interviewer's Microsoft Outlook calendar, as an appointment with a Microsoft Teams link, and you are invited to it at that email address. AMI holds the time you chose, and any change or cancellation, with your application. The calendar entry stays in the interviewer's calendar as a record that the interview was arranged.

Your membership record. Your name, membership number, grade, employer, job title, the date you joined and whether the membership is currently active. This is the record membership consists of, and holding it is how AMI can answer the question a client or a duty holder is really asking, which is whether you are who you say you are.

Your CPD. Every activity recorded against your name: what it was, when it happened, how many points it carried, whether it was accepted, and how it came to be recorded, meaning whether you declared it yourself, whether AMI awarded it automatically or whether a member of staff entered it. Any evidence you upload is held with it. The record of how each point arrived is what makes a published CPD figure worth anything, which is why it is kept rather than smoothed away.

What you write on the forum. Your posts, and the earlier versions of a post you have edited. A post is visible to every other member and shows your name, your grade and your employer beside it. Earlier versions are kept so that a discussion somebody has relied on cannot be quietly rewritten afterwards.

Your photograph. Only if you upload one. Your browser cuts the two pictures AMI uses and sends only those, so the original file never leaves your device and the location and camera details written into a photograph taken on a phone never reach AMI at all.

Your membership card. The card and any wallet pass on your phone, and the fact that a card was issued and when.

Your preferences. The switches on your profile page, including whether your record can be looked up publicly and whether your photograph appears there.

A record of changes. AMI records what changed on your record, when, and who changed it. That log cannot be edited or deleted by anybody, including AMI staff, which is the point of it: a record of decisions that can be quietly amended is not a record of anything.

Messages sent to you. Letters about your own membership, such as the acknowledgement of your application and the decision on it, are kept with a note of when they were sent.

Enquiries sent through the contact form. Your name, email address and message, which are emailed to the office. A copy of that email, and of the acknowledgement sent back to you, is kept in the office's record of what AMI has sent, so that the office can see an enquiry was answered.

What appears publicly

The public register and the verification page exist so that somebody relying on your membership can check it. Nothing appears there unless your membership is currently active.

Always shown for an active member: your name, any post nominals, your membership number and your grade.

Shown unless you switch it off: your employer, your joining date and your CPD position. The switch is on your profile page and takes effect immediately.

Shown only if you switch it on: your photograph on the public page. Your photograph appears on your own membership card by default, because that is what a card is for, and appearing on the public page is a separate decision that is off until you make it.

Never shown: your date of birth, your private address, your telephone number, your email address, your application, your documents and anything a member of staff has written about you.

If your membership stops being active, the verification page says plainly that you are not a current member, and it stops showing your photograph, your employer and your CPD position at the same moment. Your name, membership number and grade remain, because those three are what let somebody holding an old card of yours get an honest answer about it rather than a broken link.

Why AMI is allowed to hold it

Your application, your membership record, your CPD, your card and your forum posts are held because they are what performing your membership consists of. Without them there is no membership to have.

The public register and the verification page are held on the basis of AMI's legitimate interests and those of the people who rely on the register. A professional body that cannot be asked whether somebody is a member is not doing the one thing it exists to do. Your control over what appears there is how that interest is balanced against yours, and you can object at any time.

Anything AMI sends you that is not about your own membership is sent only with your consent, and you can withdraw it at any time without giving a reason and without it affecting your membership. Messages about your own membership are not marketing and are not something you can turn off, because they are how AMI tells you things you need to know.

How long AMI keeps it

Membership records and CPD records are kept indefinitely. AMI does not delete them when a membership lapses, and it has not set a number of years after which they go.

The reason is that a membership record is evidence about a point in the past. A member may need to show what their standing was in 2019 long after they have stopped being a member, and somebody investigating work done in 2019 may need the same thing. A record that has been deleted cannot answer either question, and the answer cannot be reconstructed afterwards.

That is a decision AMI has taken rather than an obligation it is under, and it is stated here so that you know about it rather than discover it. If you think your record should not be kept, you can object, and the section on your rights says what happens then.

Forum posts are kept for as long as the discussion is useful. An application that was refused, and the documents attached to it, are kept so that AMI can answer questions about the decision. An enquiry through the contact form, and the acknowledgement sent to you, are kept for 12 months and then deleted automatically.

Who else sees it

AMI does not sell your information to anybody, does not share it with advertisers and does not use it to profile you.

The site runs on services provided by other companies, and each of them handles some of your information in order to provide that service and for no other purpose: Cloudflare, which serves the site and protects it; Supabase, which stores the database and the files; SMTP2GO, which delivers email; and Microsoft, whose Outlook calendar and Teams hold your interview appointment if you book one. Recorded webinars are hosted by Vimeo, so watching one involves Vimeo. Adding your membership card to a wallet on your phone involves Apple or Google.

AMI may share information where it is required to by law.

AMI has discussed sharing certain information with ARCA, the Asbestos Removal Contractors Association. Nothing is shared today, and nothing will be until AMI's Council has decided the principle and this notice has been updated to say exactly what would be shared and why.

What you can ask for

You can see what AMI holds about you. Most of it is on your profile page and the Download my data button gives you the whole of it as a file. You can also ask the office and AMI will provide it within a month.

You can correct anything that is wrong. Some of it you can change yourself on your profile page. For the rest, tell the office and it will be put right.

You can ask AMI to delete your information. AMI will delete what it can and will tell you plainly what it is keeping and why, rather than agreeing and quietly keeping it. Where a record is evidence about the past, AMI is likely to want to keep it, and it will say so and give you its reasons.

You can object to how AMI is using your information, including its being on the public register and its being kept indefinitely. An objection is considered properly and answered, not filed.

You can ask for your information in a portable form, which is what the Download my data button already gives you.

You can withdraw consent to anything you consented to, at any time.

None of these costs anything and none of them affects your membership.

If you are not happy

Tell the office first, because most of what goes wrong is a mistake somebody can put right the same day. If that does not resolve it, you have the right to complain to the Information Commissioner's Office, which is the UK's independent regulator for data protection, at ico.org.uk. You can complain to the ICO without telling AMI first, and doing so does not affect your membership.

How AMI protects it

Every request for information is checked against who is asking, in the database itself rather than in the page you are looking at, so a page with a bug in it still cannot show you somebody else's record. Files you upload are private and are served only after those same checks. The office's administration area is behind a separate sign in. Anything you type that reaches another person's screen is treated as untrusted and cannot become part of the page.

No system is perfect, and anybody who says otherwise is selling something. What AMI can say is that it tests these things rather than assuming them, and that an independent security test is carried out before the site holds real member information.

Cookies

This site sets no advertising cookies and no analytics cookies, and there is nothing here that follows you to another site. Signing in stores a token in your browser so that you stay signed in, which is what signing in means, and it goes when you sign out.
