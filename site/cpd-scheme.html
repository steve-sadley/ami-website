Continuing Professional Development

Membership says somebody was competent when they were assessed. CPD is what keeps that statement true.

The requirement

Ten points a year, five of them core

Members at the grades that carry a CPD requirement record ten points across the year. At least five of those must come from core activities, which are the ones about asbestos technical competence. The remaining five come from the wider professional skills around the work.

The split matters more than the total. Ten points earned entirely from committee service and trade press would say nothing about whether somebody is still current on the technical work, which is the thing a client is relying on.

The year runs to the calendar year. Points are logged as activities happen rather than gathered up in December, and a member can see where they stand at any point.

Earning points

What counts

Core activities

Asbestos technical competence. At least five points a year must come from this list.

  • Assessed training course
  • Unassessed training course
  • Technical seminar or conference
  • AMI webinar or recorded session
  • Reading technical guidance, an approved code or a standard
  • Site based learning or shadowing
  • A technical assessment or examination passed

Non core activities

The wider professional skills around the technical work.

  • Regional or branch meeting
  • Committee or working group service
  • Mentoring or supervising somebody
  • Writing or presenting on a technical subject
  • Professional reading and trade press
  • Management, commercial or communication training
  • An in house briefing or toolbox talk delivered
Recording

How a point gets onto the record

The member logs it

A member records an activity from their own CPD screen, choosing the type and saying what they did. It arrives pending.

The office accepts it

Nothing counts towards a total until the AMI office has accepted it. A member cannot accept their own entry, and cannot decide whether it is core.

Some points arrive on their own

Where AMI arranged the activity and knows who attended, the points are awarded automatically and need no evidence from the member.

Every point says how it arrived

The record holds the source of each point as well as the point itself, so a line can always answer where it came from, whether the member declared it or AMI awarded it. That is the difference between a CPD figure and a list of claims, and it is the reason a figure can be published at all.

🔒 Members only

Your own record

Members log activities, see where they stand against this year's requirement and print a personal CPD record at any time, from the CPD screen behind the member login.

The printed record lists every activity with its date, its type and how the point was recorded, which is what an employer or an assessor is usually asking for.
